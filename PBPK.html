PBPK Modeling of Cetuximab Distribution in Plasma and Tumor Tissue

Two-compartment PBPK model to simulate antibody transport, binding, and clearance in tumor vs plasma.

Project

Timeline

Tools Used

Antibody PBPK Model

August-December 2024

Python (SciPy, Seaborn, Matplotlib, NumPy)

Overview

In this project, I developed a physiologically-based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) two-compartment model to simulate the distribution and clearance of cetuximab, a therapeutic antibody targeting EGFR, in a mouse bearing a solid tumor. The model describes antibody transport between blood and tumor, receptor binding, internalization, degradation, and systemic clearance. This work demonstrates my ability to integrate biological mechanisms, mathematical modeling, and computational simulation into a reproducible research workflow.

Technical contributions

  • Model formulation:
    • Constructed a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) representing antibody dynamics in plasma and tumor interstitium.
    • Incorporated processes including permeability-limited transport, receptor-mediated binding, antibody clearance, receptor synthesis, and internalization.
  • Mathematical implementation:
    • Derived binding equations for free antibody in plasma, free antibody in tumor, receptor-bound antibody in tumor, and EGFR receptor concentration.
    • Parameterized rate constants using literature values (association/dissociation kinetics, clearance half-life, receptor density, tumor volume, Krogh's cylinder geometry).
  • Computational simulation:
    • Implemented the model in Python (NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn) within a Jupyter notebook.
    • Solved stiff ODEs using solve_ivp with BDF integration, ensuring numerical stability across multiple timescales.
    • Designed simulations for both single bolus injection and multi-dose regiments, enabling exploration of dosing strategies.
  • Data visualization & analysis:
    • Generated time-course plots of antibody distribution across compartments (plasma vs tumor, bound vs free states).
    • Illustrated pharmacokinetic features such as biphasic decay, tumor penetration kinetics, and receptor saturation thresholds.
    • Explored how changing model parameters (e.g. off-rate koff, clearance half-life, receptor internalization rate) influences therapeutic efficacy.

Key findings

  • A single 30µg injection was insufficient to saturate tumor EGFR receptors, as the bound antibody concentration remained significantly lower than receptor availability.
  • Increasing dosage (10x) or adjusting pharmacokinetic parameters (lower koff, slower clearance, slower internalization) substantially improved receptor occupancy and tumor saturation.
  • Multi-dose regiments (30µg every 3 days × 4 doses) increased exposure but still fell short of full saturation, highlighting the importance of affinity optimization and dosing strategies.

Impact

This project highlights my expertise at the intersection of bioengineering, pharmacology, and computational modeling. By building a PBPK model from first principles, parameerizing it with experimental data, and simulating realistic treatment regimens, I demonstrated the power of in silico modeling to inform antibody dosing strategies, predict therapeutic efficacy, and guide experimental design in translational oncology.
