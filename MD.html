MD Simulation with Velocity Verlet Algorithm

An investigation into the energy conservation behaviors of a 3D MD simulation of Hydrogen atoms in a box using velocity Verlet.

Background

This molecular dynamics simulation was originally provided as a homework assignment by Prof. Robert Riggleman as part of a Molecular Modeling course (CBE525) at the University of Pennsylvania. I have extended the original simulation framework and analyzed the code to deepen my understanding of computational molecular modeling and numerical simulation techniques. This project implements a high-performance Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulator in C, modeling a 3D system of particles interacting via the Lennard-Jones potential in the canonical (NVT) ensemble. The simulation tracks atomic motion, energy evolution, and temperature fluctuations over time using an efficient, physics-grounded approach. My broader work includes MCMC techniques, which I've used in other projects.

Key features:

  • Velocity Verlet integrator

    • Accurate time integration scheme preserving time-reversibility and energy conservation, suitable for Hamiltonian systems.

  • Periodic Boundary Conditions (PBCs)

    • Implements minimum image convention to replicate an infinite bulk system while simulating only a finite number of particles.

  • Cell List-Based Neighbor Search

    • Improves computational efficiency from O(N2) to approximately O(N) by spatially decomposing the simulation box into neighbor cells.

  • Lennard-Jones Potential with Energy Shift

    • Models short-range repulsive and long-range attractive interactions. Includes energy shifting for continuity at cutoff.

  • Force Calculation and Validation

    • Forces are analytically derived and validated via numerical differentiation, ensuring accuracy of particle dynamics.

  • Real-Time Thermodynamic Monitoring

    • Calculates kinetic energy, potential energy, total energy, and instantaneous temperature every N steps. Logs to log.dat for downstream analysis.

  • XYZ Trajectory Output

    • Outputs atomic positions in .xyz format compatible with visualization tools like Ovito, VMD, or PyMOL for trajectory animation.

  • Flexible Initialization

    • Option to initialize particles on a 3D grid or from custom configurations (via input.config file).

    Random Gaussian-distributed velocities with center-of-mass velocity correction.

  • Modular Design

    • Core routines for force computation, PBCs, random number generation, and output are clearly modularized and reusable.
