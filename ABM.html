ABM Model of Wilms' Tumor in PhysiCell

An investigation into chemotherapy efficacy on heterogeneous Wilms' tumor subtypes.

Project

Timeline

Tools Used

Master's Thesis

August 2024 - May 2025

Python, C, PhysiCell, MATLAB, Linux

For my Master’s thesis in Bioengineering, I developed and validated an agent-based model (ABM) to investigate how tumor composition and the tumor microenvironment influence chemotherapy efficacy in Wilms tumor, a pediatric kidney cancer. This project combined computational modeling, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) analysis, and systems biology approaches to explore how blastemal versus regressive cell subtypes differentially respond to treatment under varying microenvironmental stresses such as hypoxia, acidosis, and nutrient deprivation.

Technical Contributions

  • Model development: Built a 2D agent-based model in PhysiCell version 1.14.1 to simulate tumor spheroid growth under different regressive-to-blastemal cell ratios. Integrated cell behaviors (growth, motility, adhesion, apoptosis) with environmental gradients of oxygen, nutrients, and chemotherapeutics.
  • PK/PD integration: Parameterized drug transport and cytotoxicity for three frontline Wilms tumor chemotherapies (doxorubicin, vincristine, actinomycin D) using literature-derived values for diffusion coefficients, uptake rates, and IC50 thresholds.
  • Spatiotemporal simulation: Implemented Hill-function–based rules to capture dynamic effects of hypoxia and acidosis on drug resistance, cell motility, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transitions (EMT).
  • Data analysis & validation: Ran multi-condition simulations with stochastic replicates, applied statistical testing (t-tests, Kruskal-Wallis, Dunn’s post-hoc), and compared computational trends to clinical data.
  • Visualization: Created time-series plots, violin plots, and heatmaps to illustrate treatment outcomes and tumor evolution across subtypes.

Key findings:

  • Higher blastemal composition correlates with increased chemoresistance, supporting clinical observations.
  • Doxorubicin preferentially depleted regressive cells, while blastemal cells persisted, shifting tumor composition toward more aggressive phenotypes.
  • Hypoxic gradients promoted EMT-like behaviors and chemotaxis toward oxygen sources, reinforcing therapy resistance.

Impact

This project demonstrated the potential of in silico tumor modeling to predict therapy outcomes, guide experimental design, and inform clinical strategies for high-risk pediatric cancers. It also highlights my ability to bridge biology, computation, and engineering principles to solve translational biomedical problems.
